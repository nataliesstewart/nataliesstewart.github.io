I've spent much of the last year working on a series of six papers on the topic of _interchange_ in the kinds of (higher) algebraic structures that pop up in equivariant homotopy theory. 
Primary examples include (homotopy coherent) incomplete Mackey functors, bi-incomplete Tambara functors, \(V\)-fold loop \(G\)-spaces, and their Thom \(G\)-spectra.
Since each paper is written to present an independent narrative, I figured it's a good idea to explain the coherent motivation of the five here. 
They all center the following metamathematical question.

**Question.** What are the algebraic analogues of $V$-loop space theory?

Secretly, while answering this, I'm always holding a silent secondary motivations of developing the \(G\)-operadic framework enough to handle Real trace methods in the noncommutative setting and to develop Basterra-Mandell obstruction theory for \(\mathbb{E}_V\)-ring spectra.

## Stable \(G\)-homotopy theory

Let \(G\) be a compact Lie group.
A prominant motivation of equivariant loop spaces comes from the embedding theorem proved by [Mostow](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/mostow) and [Palais](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/palais.pdf):
every smooth manifold with smooth \(G\)-action (henceforth _smooth \(G\)-manifold) admits a smooth equivariant embedding into a (potentially very high-dimensional) orthogonal representation, provided its points have isotropy only falling in *finitely many* conjugacy classes of \(G\).
For instance, this always holds if \(G\) is finite or the manifold is compact.

In the same way that e.g. the Whitney embedding theorem powers atiyah duality (giving a notion of stable normal bundle of a compact smooth manifold, whose thom spectrum ends up dual to its suspension spectrum via a Pontryagin-Thom collapse), [Wirthmüller](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/wirtmul1.pdf) recognized that Mostow-Palais' theorem yields an equivariant stable normal bundle whose Thom \(G\)-spectrum dualizes the image of a compact smooth \(G\)-manifold in the version of the Spanier-Whitehead category that inverts smashing with orthogonal \(G\)-representations;
the associated (twisted) self duality of the "suspepnsion \(G\)-spectra" is the celebrated *Wirthmüller* isomorphism
\[
	[G/H]^{\vee}_+ \simeq G_+ \wedge_{H+} S^{-\mathrm{Res}_H^G \mathrm{adj}(G)},
\]
where \(\mathrm{adj}\) is the adjoint representation and \(S^{- \bullet}\) takes a formal inverse of the (equivariant) one-point compactification of an orthogonal representation.
In the case that \(G\) is discrete (hence finite),  this adjoint representaton term is trivial, so the right hand side ends up trivial, expressing \([G/H]_+\) as being honest-to-god self-dual.

Of course, for formal nonsense reasons, the ind-completion of this Spanier- Whitehead category ends up equivalent to the *right-handed* construction
\[
	\mathrm{Sp}_G \coloneqq \mathrm{lim}_V \mathrm{S}_{G,  *},
\]
which is a limit taken along \(\Omega^V = \mathrm{Map}(S^V, -)\) as \(V\)-ranges across the finite-dimensional orthogonal \(G\)-representations infinitely many times.
Said in another way, an object in the Ind-completion (which ought be viewed as "\(G\)"spectra, hence their name) consists of a space with distinguished \(V\)-deloopings for all finite-dimensional orthogonal representations \(V\), functorially under inclusions of subrepresentations.
In particular, if you're going to try to find a category whose objects represent equivariant cohomology theories, \(G\)-spectra will be a universal choice.

We can power this by examples, of course;
for instance, if \(G\) is finite, then every finite-dimension orthogonal \(G\)-representation embeds into a multiple of the *regular* representation (i.e. \(\rho \coloneqq \mathbb{R}[G/e]\)), so a \(G\)-spectrum is equivalently a sequence of equivalences \[
	X_0 \simeq \Omega^{\rho} X_1 \simeq \Omega^{2 \rho} \simeq \cdots.
\]
In this language, we find \(G\)-spectra in nature! 
For instance, Atiyah's [Real bott periodicity](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/atiyahkr.pdf) recognizes \(BU_{\mathbb{R}}\) as a \(\rho\)-periodic \(C_2\)-space, so it corresponds with a \(C_2\) spectrum \(KU_{\mathbb{R}}\) called *Real topological \(K\)-theory*;
the old-school refers to this as \(K\mathbb{R}\)-theory.

Now, how do we construct other examples?
Nonequivariantly, we can apply the [recognition principle](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~may/BOOKS/gils.pdf) to reduce the problem of constructing infinite deloopings (subject to additional connectivity assumptions) to constructing highly structured multiplications on our space.
In the equivariant setting, we will need something *sophisticated* to construct these deloopings, as it's not clear a priori how the delooping \(B^V\) should work in general.
We require *\(V\)-loop space theory*.

## \(V\)-loop space theory

[Rourke and Sanderson](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9712216) are perhaps the main progenitors of equivariant loop space theory;
their approximation theorem in essence exhibited a "\(G\)-operad" whose algebras in pointed connected \(G\)-spaces correspond with (necessarily unique) \(V+1)\-connected \(G\)-spaces \(Y\) and equivalences \(\Omega^V Y \simeq X\).

This brings up an obvious question: 
what about infinite loop spaces?
Well, if $V$ has positive-dimensional fixed points, then it doesn't seem to hard to construct an equivalence
\[
  \Sp\Alg_{\mathbb{E_{\infty V}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{G,*}\right) \simeq \mathrm{lim} \left( \cdots \xrightarrow{\Omega^V} \mathcal{S}_{G,*} \xrightarrow{\Omega^V} \mathcal{S}_{G,*}\right),
\]
i.e. pointed connected $\mathbb{E}_{\infty V}$-algebras should present a model for $\Omega^V$-spectra.

Some context-laying is in order:
it follows from the beautiful work of [Schlank and Yanovski](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.06006) that, in the case \(G = e\), this is somewhat unavoidable on the side of algebra;
for all reduced operads $\mathcal{O}$ other than $\mathbb{E}_0$, there is a unique natural equivalence
\[
  \cdots \Alg_{\cO} \Alg_{\cO}(\cC) \simeq \CAlg(\cC),
\]
where the former denotes the limit of $n$-fold iterates $\Alg_{\cO} \cdots \Alg_{\cO}(\cC)$ with maps from $n$-fold iterate to $(n-1)$-fold iterate forgetting the outermost structure.
In particular, if we found some exotic sort of loop space theory with a corepresenting reduced operad, in the infinite case, it would just spit out spectra again. 

This argument is facilitated by a complete classification of the idempotent reduced operads;
the left hand side of the above equivalence is corepresented by the infinite Boardman-Vogt tensor product $\mathcal{O}^{\otimes \infty}$, so the above statement is equivalent to the claim that the $\otimes$-idempotent reduced operads are precisely $\mathbb{E}_0$ and $\mathbb{E}_\infty$.
We're inspired to ask the following questions.

**Questions.** Is homotopy-coherent interghance of $G$-operad algebras corepresented by a "Boardman-Vogt tensor product?" What are the idempotent $G$-operads, and what are their algebras? How quickly do iterated tensor products of $G$-operads approach their corresponding idempotent, and are $\mathbb{E}_V$-operads closed under tensor products?

To begin to address this, we must regress to humble beginnings;
we start with the combinatorics of equivariant arity.

## Equivariant arity and weak indexing systems

In nonequivariant algbera, we often refer to _\(n\)-ary operations_;
nullary operations are constants, unary are endomorphisms, binary are maps \(X^2 \rightarrow X\), etc.
An important example of this is the operations on a symmetric monoidal category:
the unit is the nullary operation, and the \(n\)-fold tensor product is the \(n\)-ary operation. 
In practice, we often like to describe the relationship of operations with the permutation $\Sigma_n$-action on an $n$-tuple of inputs, so we instead speak of \(S\)-ary operations, where $S$ is a finite set, acted upon by isomorphisms of finite sets. 

To make a long story shortish, [Hill-Hopkins-Ravanel](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3724) wanted to make some stuff to do some things, and they found it useful to define a _norm functor_ $N_H^G\colon \mathrm{Sp}_H \rightarrow \mathrm{Sp}_G$.
They also used the pre-existing tensor product of $G$-spectra, and these combine beautifully to form an _$S$-indexed tensor functor_
\[
  \bigotimes^S\colon \mathrm{Sp}_S \rightarrow \mathrm{Sp}_G;
\]
here, $\mathrm{Sp}_S \coloneqq \prod_{[G/H] \subset S} \mathrm{S}_H$.
This kicked off a perspective on equivariant algebra;
*the arity of operations landing in $G$-equivariant objects must be finite $G$-sets.*

However, this doesn't describe the whole shebang;
for instance, it is hard from this perspective to encapsulate the natural equivalence \(N_K^H N_H^G \simeq N_K^G\).
Using the "induction" equivalence \(\mathbb{F}_H \simeq \mathbb{F}_{G, /[G/H]}\), we colloquially define an "equivariant arity" to be a finite \(H\)-set, for some conjucagy class \((H) \subset G\).
