This is a short rambly manifesto about model categories and equivariant algebra, meant to be only somewhat inflammatory. 

###  Changing my mind
Those who were present in the Cambridge MA homotopy theory community for a significant portion of the years 2021-2023 may remember that I spent those years as a younger grad student, utterly unconvinced that we had sufficiently advanced \(\infty\)-categorical machinery to practice homotopy-coherent algebra without carrying around model structures from time to time;
those who know me now will know that this is _far_ from how I think, speak, and write about mathematics now.
So what happened?

<p>
Lower bounds on the volumes of hyperbolic link complements are given via a new construction:
  a <i> bracelet link</i> is a link in \(S^3\) decomposed as a cycle of interconnected tangles, and it is proved that a bracelet link of \(2n\) tangles, such that each individual tangle may be <i>replicated</i> into a hyperbolic bracelet link of \(2n\) copies of the tangle, is hyperbolic, with volume at least the average of the replicated links.
</p>

  This replication is generalized to arbitrary 3-manifolds via a construction called <i>starbursts</i>, which separate the manifold into <i>pieces</i>, which have a well defined \(2n\)-<i>replicant</i>;
  if the resulting pieces from removing a regular neighborhood of a starburst have hyperbolic replicants, then the 3-manifold is hyperbolic, with volume at least the average of the volumes of the replicants. 
</p>
<p>
  Applications are presented to hyperbolicity of links in thickened surfaces and in the solid torus.
</p>

<summary>e \(\infty\)-categorical framework gelled well with specialization to the 1-categorical case, because I was concerned about functoriality of operads under change of the _enriching_ category, which is hard to implement from the May-Thomason _category of operators_ approach that dominates the higher-categorical operadic literature; principally, to enrich this approach requires an enriched theory of cocartesian fibrations, which seems like more work than it's worth.
2. I thought that Lurie's model categorical framework of _categorical patterns_ was indispensible for more difficult arguments surrounding operads, such as practically computing homotopical Boardman-Vogt tensor products.
2.  I didn't think that the general technique of constructing _explicit_ cofibrant replacements (be it cell structures, projective resolutions, or whatever else) could be managed in a purely homotopical setting.

For the first point, I was convinced by the beautiful comparison work of [Cisinski-Moerdijk](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.4956)/[Barwick](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5756)/[Chu-Haugseng-Heuts](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03826) (and subsequent simplification by [Hinich-Moerdijk](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.14033)) that this is compatible with a multi-modal approach, passing to (\(\mathbb{E}_1\)-)monoids in symmetric sequences when considering algebraic or spectral operads specifically;
moreover, for understanding the relationship to things like power operations (which are not so delicate from a categorical perspective as enriched operads), it suffices to note that the associated monad can easily be described using (\(\mathcal{O}(n)-twisted\)-)symmetric powers, the description of which can be pushed around without too much difficulty.

For the second, I simply came to learn that the algebraic patterns approach of [Chu-Haugseng](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03977)/[Barkan-Haugseng-Steinebrunner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.07183) obviates categorical patterns, and much of the basic study of operads can be cleanly done in the equifibered approach of [Barkan-Steinebrunner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02576) (such as the study of the [homotopy d-operad](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04061), which was easy to [equivariantize](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02129) via the equifibered approach).

For the third, [Ishan Levy](https://ishanina.github.io/) patiently taught me that Bondarko's [weight structures](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.4003) are essentially \(\infty\)-categorical in nature (to the same degree that $t$-structures are), mentioning that this technology recovers both the stupid filtration and the skeletal filtration; this allows one to define variants of e.g. the Atiyah-Hirzebruch spectral sequence simply via the weight spectral sequence, avoiding serious study of a point-set model.

### Becoming frustrated

It's safe to say that, by the time I started seriously thinking about equivariant homotopy theory, I was convinced that the most efficient way to do so would be to develop technology purely \(\infty\)-categorically, and to perform computations by some combination of Dwyer-Kan style construction of objects/maps and essentially \(\infty\)-categorical uses of spectral sequences, homological functors, etc. for calculating invariants of those objects/ maps.

Unfortunately for the purist, math is an essentially social discipline, and it is counterproductive to cloister yourself;
if you do equivariant homotopy theory, then nowadays it's still best practice to maintain fluency in model category theory and to carry around point set models whenever you can. 
If you can't read model category theory, then you will have no idea what is and isn't known about \(G\)-operads. Indeed, as of writing (excluding my own work), I know of exactly four papers which contribute to the May-Thomason style model for \(G\) (of which one is model categorical), yet the well springs forth with model categorical papers about genuine \(G\)-operads (see papers such as Guiterrez-White.
